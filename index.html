Game design philosophy (from the master plan)

Shinobi Legacy should become a living shinobi world rather than a level-grinding loop. Players begin as Academy students, discover their chakra, graduate, build relationships with a persistent AI squad, complete missions, rise through ranks, explore forgotten places, forge and bond with legendary weapons, master summons and elements, build homes and organizations, compete with other players, and uncover hidden stories. A player's identity should come from choices, skill, exploration, relationships, and accomplishments, not rarity alone.

1. Academy, AI squad & shinobi progression

Core objective: redesign the new-player journey. Every first-time character begins as an Academy Student, discovers chakra natures, graduates, receives a persistent AI squad, and rises through the shinobi ranks.

1.1 First-time Academy experience

Spawn new characters inside the Ninja Academy of their selected village instead of directly into the open world.

Build explorable interiors and exteriors: classrooms, training grounds, instructor offices, practice areas, and hallways.

Populate the Academy with AI students, instructors, and NPCs. Academy uniforms, interactive dialogue, tutorials, and activities.

Introduce the player to their instructor through a cinematic opening interaction.

Returning players bypass the Academy introduction and never repeat it.

1.2 Five-stage graduation examination

Written exam: shinobi history, chakra fundamentals, combat knowledge, village knowledge, and tactical decision-making.

Chakra control exam: balance, precision, designated surfaces, and a visible chakra meter.

Clone exam: perform the required clone technique and meet the clone-count and execution requirements.

Practical exam: playable obstacle course, movement, combat practice, and training targets.

Final evaluation: controlled fight against an Academy instructor, then a performance evaluation.

Passing promotes the player to Genin with a graduation cutscene, announcement, profile update, and rank display. Players who fail can retake it. Every stage must be playable, not merely a cutscene.

1.3 Chakra nature discovery and mastery

Remove the paid/spin/roll/reroll element system from character creation and inventory.

During the Academy chakra demonstration, an assessment object reacts, and the game assigns TWO different basic natures at equal odds, without duplicates: Fire, Water, Wind, Lightning, and Earth Release.

Save both natures permanently and show them on the player profile and chakra information screen.

Each nature has independent mastery: Beginner, Intermediate, Advanced, Expert, Master. Mastery unlocks techniques progressively; owning a nature does not grant every related technique.

Clan abilities, bloodlines, and special transformations stay separate from ordinary nature discovery.

1.4 AI-generated three-person squad and Sensei

After graduation, generate one squad with the player, two AI teammates, and one AI Sensei, plus a squad identity and number.

Each teammate has a unique name, appearance, hairstyle, outfit, clan, two chakra natures, combat specialization, personality, strengths, weaknesses, backstory, dialogue, and relationship state.

Personality archetypes: Prodigy, Strategist, Quiet One, Reckless One, Support, Rival. Personality affects dialogue, choices, relationships, and combat decisions.

Generate the squad only once; persist names, appearance, personalities, clan, natures, relationships, progression, and squad number across sessions.

Each Sensei has a unique appearance, name, personality, rank, combat style, special abilities, dialogue, and backstory. The Sensei introduces the squad, assigns missions, teaches techniques, organizes training, evaluates performance, reacts to decisions, advises, joins selected missions, and tracks progress.

1.5 Squad bonding and combat AI

Conversations, friendship progression, rivalries, training interactions, shared mission memories, reactions to decisions, combination attacks, personal storylines, and relationship-based dialogue.

NPCs remember important events and reference earlier interactions.

Teammates follow the player, fight independently, target enemies, use their own jutsu, dodge, block, support, protect, and coordinate attacks.

Combat decisions weigh enemy position, health, chakra, distance, and current battle conditions. Roles: Offensive, Defensive, Support, Tactical.

Coordinated squad attacks with synchronized animations and effects.

1.6 Missions, Chunin Selection, and Rank Progression

Mission tiers: D-Rank village activities; C-Rank low-risk field missions; B-Rank advanced operations; A-Rank high-level assignments; S-Rank elite missions.

Mission types: escort, reconnaissance, tracking, enemy encounters, protection, retrieval, investigation, and boss encounters. AI teammates participate. Rewards: experience, reputation, mission rewards, and rank eligibility.

Chunin Selection stages: written examination; survival examination in a large objective-based environment; preliminary matches against AI candidates; final tournament in an arena with spectators, village leaders, announcers, and cinematic introductions; leadership evaluation.

Promotion depends on rank, missions, teamwork, combat, decision-making, and leadership. Not every candidate passes; failed candidates can try again.

Rank path: Academy Student → Genin → Chunin → Jonin → Special Jonin → ANBU → Kage-Level. Every rank has unique requirements, missions, unlocks, responsibilities, profile updates, and NPC interactions. High-ranking players may lead AI squads, train Academy students, and join advanced village operations.

1.7 Living Academy and rank-aware world

Academy schedules, NPC daily routines, courtyard training, classroom instruction, graduation ceremonies, squad introductions, village announcements, exam events, and mission-board interactions.

Academy students recognize graduates; village residents acknowledge Genin and Chunin; high-ranking players get special recognition.

1.8 New UI panels

Academy Progress; Examination Results; Chakra Natures; Chakra Mastery; Squad Information; Teammate Profiles; Sensei Information; Squad Relationships; Mission Assignments; Rank Progression.

Cinematic transitions for chakra discovery, graduation, squad assignment, Chunin promotion, and major examination results. Match the existing Shinobi Legacy identity and avoid clutter.

2. Ultimate world expansion

Core objective: build a connected shinobi MMORPG with deeper activities, exploration, crafting, factions, multiplayer, and legendary discoveries.

2.1 Summoning contracts

Species: snakes, toads, slugs, wolves, hawks, dragons, foxes, ancient mythical creatures, and original legendary species, each with unique models, animations, personality, summoning sequence, combat/passive abilities, rarity, and progression.

Discover contracts through scrolls, quests, trials, guardians, and compatibility tests.

Mastery unlocks stronger summons, commands, transformations, combined attacks, and larger summons. Summons follow and fight alongside owners. Dedicated interface.

2.2 Stealth and infiltration

Crouching, cover, sneaking, disguises, distractions, and stealth objectives.

NPC detection weighs distance, visibility, movement, noise, disguise, and suspicious behavior. Detection states: Unaware, Suspicious, Investigating, Alerted, Combat.

Missions: document recovery, prisoner rescue, scroll recovery, intelligence gathering, facility entry, and undetected escapes. Include alarms, guard communication, patrol routes, and multiple completion paths.

2.3 Advanced training simulator

Skill-based chakra control, speed/reaction, obstacle, weapon, projectile, sword, combat, blocking, dodging, counter, combo, and hand-sign challenges.

Facilities: academies, private rooms, village grounds, advanced mastery spaces, and multiplayer arenas. Reward demonstrated mastery, not button clicks.

2.4 Weapon forging and customization

Craft katanas, swords, spears, scythes, daggers, staffs, specialized shinobi weapons, and legendary weapons.

Gather ores, ancient materials, boss drops, fragments, and elemental materials. Use blacksmith NPCs or crafting stations.

Customize colors, handles, blades, decorations, effects, engravings, and names. Attributes may include durability, elemental affinity, chakra efficiency, attack specialization, and passives.

Legendary weapons have histories, compatibility quests, awakenings, abilities, models, and cinematics.

2.5 Secret organizations

Underground groups with identities, symbols, uniforms, hideouts, leaders, members, rank structures, exclusive missions, and rewards.

Recruitment may require reputation, achievements, clan requirements, hidden quests, or trials. Members gain ranks, techniques, secret access, and faction operations.

Organizations compete over resources, territories, information, and influence.

2.6 Player-owned properties

Buy village homes, residences, estates, clan compounds, private training facilities, and hidden hideouts.

Customize interiors, furniture, weapon/trophy displays, guest access, training areas, and upgrades. Clan compounds include meeting rooms, training, storage, symbols, and private entrances.

Persist ownership and customization; prevent duplication and unauthorized access.

2.7 Chakra and elemental mastery

Expand the existing six-element ecosystem (Fire, Water, Wind, Earth, Lightning, Yin) while preserving existing jutsu and compatibility.

Train through technique use, challenges, quests, trials, and scrolls. Compatible advanced combinations may include Ice, Lava, Storm, Wood, Magnetism, Scorch, Steam, Crystal, and original combinations.

Require eligibility and mastery; unlock effects, jutsu, control, transformations, and animations. Do not grant every combination automatically.

2.8 Traveling merchants

Merchants travel between villages, marketplaces, remote routes, and hidden locations on schedules.

Inventory: scrolls, materials, cosmetics, consumables, summoning items, crafting resources, and limited collectibles. Secret merchants appear during weather/celestial events, in abandoned settlements, or underground. Rotate stock without deleting purchased items.

2.9 Prison and captivity

Capture connects to village security and rogue-shinobi gameplay: capture/transport events, notifications, records, security NPCs, prison quests, prisoner interactions, escape opportunities, recovery, and release.

Escapes use puzzles, teamwork, abilities, passages, or timed events. Prevent endless imprisonment, spawn camping, repeated targeting, and abusive capture loops.

2.10 Dynamic mission generator

Expand escort, investigation, NPC combat, rescue, defense, exploration, intelligence, rogue shinobi, boss hunt, and emergency village missions.

Randomize locations, enemy formations, objectives, optional challenges, completion methods, and branching outcomes. Scale by rank, progression, group size, category, and enemy difficulty.

Outcomes may affect reputation, relationships, world events, quests, and organizations.

2.11 Multiplayer and competitive play

Clan wars: registration, declarations, battle sites, teams, scoring, victory conditions, rewards.

Permanent player squads: names, leaders, invitations, shared missions, progression, records, emblems.

Ranked tournaments: registration, brackets, matchmaking, spectators, history, rewards, seasons.

Territory control: ownership, points, scheduled battles, rewards, records, announcements.

Friendly duels: standard, restricted, no-transformations, ranked sparring, team rules.

Server-verified leaderboards: strength, rank, duels, clans, missions, bosses, exploration.

2.12 Secret bloodline awakening

Dormant potential discovered through NPCs, scrolls, secret places, clan quests, world events, and hidden requirements.

Awakenings have cinematics, visuals, abilities, mastery, and lore. They are earned, never random free power-ups.

2.13 The world's unknown strongest

A mysterious original, extremely powerful NPC travels the world, appears unexpectedly, avoids ordinary markers, observes players, interacts with NPCs, and disappears after encounters.

Discovery leads to dialogue and a difficult multi-phase challenge with attack patterns. Rewards may include a title, cosmetic, weapon material, technique scroll, and achievement.

2.14 Legendary weapon recognition

Weapons have personality, history, and compatibility. A weapon may accept or reject a player or test mastery. Trials can involve guardians, combat, history, and style mastery.

Bonding unlocks weapon abilities, animations, mastery, lore, and transformations.

2.15 Lost villages

Abandoned villages, destroyed clan settlements, forgotten fortresses, underground communities, and ancient training grounds.

Environmental storytelling, records, hidden NPCs, passages, lost equipment, techniques, enemies, histories, disappearance causes, and exploration rewards. Discoveries unlock quests and main-lore reveals.

2.16 Natural and legendary progression

Common-clan players can become elite through combat, training, missions, exploration, bosses, weapon mastery, elemental mastery, and competition.

Paths: legendary swordsman, master tactician, elemental specialist, summoning master, hand-to-hand specialist, forbidden-technique specialist. Accomplishments earn titles, achievements, dialogue, and recognition.

Rare clans keep unique advantages without making rarity the only path to strength.

Systems affect one another: merchants appear in lost villages; organizations offer infiltration; contracts require hidden bosses; weather reveals weapons; rivals appear at world events; leaders organize clan wars; mastery earns titles; bloodlines require exploration; NPCs recognize legendary accomplishments.

4. Permanent injury & sacrifice system

Planned / future update. Combat, character progression, and permanent consequences. The intent is meaningful non-cosmetic consequences without graphic presentation.

4.1 Body-part loss and persistence

Eligible characters may permanently lose the left arm, right arm, left eye, or right eye. Maximum: one arm and one eye.

The player may choose which side is lost where the game rules allow. Injuries persist across sessions and are stored in character data.

Integrate with combat, animation, abilities, character models, customization, and multiplayer.

4.2 Cinematic presentation

Distinct non-graphic cinematics for left/right arm loss, left/right eye loss, and combined arm-and-eye loss.

Temporary camera transitions, character reactions, aura/effect changes, dramatic sound, an injury notification, and a smooth return to gameplay.

4.3 Eye and ability consequences

Track each eye independently so losing one eye removes only abilities tied to that eye.

Rinnegan: disable abilities assigned to the lost eye and dependent techniques where appropriate. Sharingan: reduce advanced visual prediction and tracking.

Mangekyo / Eternal Mangekyo: remove the lost eye's unique abilities. Byakugan: remove enhanced vision and chakra-point perception. Tenseigan: remove abilities and transformations dependent on the lost eye. Ketsuryugan: remove ocular techniques associated with the lost eye.

4.4 Arm consequences and alternate paths

Losing the left arm disables techniques requiring it, modifies two-handed weapon animations, and restricts certain hand-sign combinations.

Losing the right arm disables techniques requiring it, modifies sword stances, and restricts dual-wielding techniques.

One-handed combat animations, modified stances, and alternative hand-sign animations where possible; disable only abilities that genuinely require the missing arm.

Alternative mastery paths: One-Handed Mastery (stances, combos, attacks); Blind Warrior (alternative perception and defense); Broken Limit (temporary amplification with cooldowns/drawbacks); Sacrifice Awakening (earned progression that does not fully replace sacrificed abilities).

4.5 Rare restoration and injury records

Rare restoration may come from legendary medical techniques, forbidden regeneration, mythical clan abilities, or difficult world quests. It must carry meaningful costs and preserve records and progression.

Injury History on profiles: body part, event date, disabled abilities, unlocked mastery, and restoration status.

Models, animations, and stances show injuries. NPCs recognize visible injuries and react through dialogue.

4.6 Technical integration

Persistent character data and modular per-body-part systems.

Connect restrictions to clan, Dojutsu, combat, and jutsu systems. Compatibility with existing character rigs and multiplayer.

Prevent exploits involving restoration, duplicate states, or bypassing ability restrictions.

Project overview

Shinobi: Life Unbound is a single-player, Naruto-inspired life sim that runs entirely in the browser. You pick a name, a body, and a village; everything else (era, surname, clan) auto-rolls, and the clan comes from three spins you roll yourself, and the game plays out a whole life: birth, family, school, career, missions, jutsu training, bloodline awakening, eras, descendants, aging and death, with the Legacy Hall keeping your line's story.
